## Background

The FSL already permits broad **free** use of this Software for any *Permitted
Purpose* — including your internal and business use, modification, and
redistribution. The one thing the FSL withholds is a *Competing Use*: making the
Software available to others in a commercial product or service that substitutes
for ours. Providing K2 to third parties as a paid product or service is therefore
**not** permitted by the FSL alone.

This grant opens exactly one such commercial channel.

## The Grant — Commercial Hosting via the official tunnel

Notwithstanding the Competing-Use restriction in the FSL, we additionally grant
you the right to **host, operate, or provide K2 as a managed or commercial
service to third parties** (for example, an MSP or agency running K2 on behalf of
its clients), **provided that ALL remote-access / tunnel functionality for that
service is provided exclusively through the official K2 Connect (a.k.a. "K2 Toge")
hosted service, under a current K2 Connect service agreement at its then-current
pricing.**

Plainly: you may build a commercial offering on top of K2 for others, so long as
the remote-access path runs through our official tunnel service and is paid for
under our then-current terms. Our pricing may change over time without affecting
this grant.

## What still requires a separate agreement with us

This grant does **not** permit:

- Hosting or providing K2 to third parties commercially where remote access does
  **not** run through the official K2 Connect service — e.g. operating your own
  tunnel/relay infrastructure to serve clients. That requires our **Enterprise
  "bring-your-own-tunnel" (BYOT)** tier or a separate commercial license.
- Anything the FSL prohibits that this grant does not expressly permit.

For the Enterprise BYOT tier — including on-prem K2 Connect for air-gapped or
regulated environments — contact us.

## Relationship to the FSL

This grant is supplemental. Except as expressly broadened above, every term of the
accompanying FSL (Permitted Purpose, Patents, Redistribution, Trademarks, and the
Grant of Future License) continues to apply unchanged. The FSL's conversion to
Apache 2.0 on the second anniversary of each release applies to the FSL text
itself; this supplemental grant governs commercial hosting in the interim.
